- At 845 m, a low marine storehouse occupies the previously weak foreground. Its roof, canopy, doors and paving make the outside of the turn read as a venue rather than only lawn with a distant facade. The road exit remains visible.
- At 670 m, the connected workshop court retains a readable route, braking boards and near/midground depth. The newer arrival/return additions do not replace this accepted sample construction with another approach.
- At night, practical road pools retain the curve, edges and vehicle separation. The new facade silhouettes remain visible, though their surfaces are subdued.
- The promenade view remains open rather than becoming a continuous opaque wall. Its old lawn, isolated planting and distant architecture remain visible quality debt.

No gross missing mesh, road penetration, opaque foliage card or obvious ground overlap was seen in these eight selected frames. ## Remaining art issues

- Workshop fronts still repeat recognizable door, sign, planter and roof patterns. Exposed surfaces remain simple at close range.
- Promenade/background lawns are broad and flat; distant facade blocks and sparse planting still look schematic. The new construction improves the service section but does not justify a universal environment-quality claim.
- Night foliage and unlit workshop faces lose considerable definition. The new depth is stronger in daylight.
- Some inherited flat road-repair shapes and the simplified water/props remain less convincing than the new material detail.
- Palm pinnae are improved, but distant crowns simplify into broader lobes. The prior verified LOD stills establish static envelope consistency, not temporal stability.
