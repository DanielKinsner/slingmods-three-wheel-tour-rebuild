# Existing SM-133 emitter calibration

One matched diagnostic sweep compared 300, 900 and 1,800 renderer nits per emitter at default UI brightness. Red and cyan were inspected with the same opaque geometry, chase pose, exposure, world materials, sun, ambient, headlights and practicals. 900 was selected: 300 was weaker; 1,800 spread farther and created an unnecessarily white cyan core. No geometry revision or global lighting adjustment followed the sweep.

Each existing side emitter remains 0.035 m × 1.3 m, facing downward. Final intensity = 900 × (saved brightness / 0.6). Thus default 60% gives 900 nits, approximately 128.65 nominal renderer lumens per side / 257.30 total. The retained 15–85% range gives 225–1,275 nits. These are artistic renderer parameters, not a measured or advertised SM-133 product rating. Color, material response and tone mapping change apparent brightness. Emissive diffuser mapping is retained.

Local near-chase diagnostic frames show distinct red/cyan ground patches at both less-lit and streetlit poses. Fine underbody/wall occlusion remains an area-light limitation: these emitters do not cast shadow maps. Existing front-strip ground contribution remains simplified. No claim of exact 2024 installation routing, physical photometry, new add-ons or upgraded driving stats is made.

The selected final stills and ordinary elapsed-time video supplement this stationary sweep. Historical pose samples are explicitly labeled diagnostic; they are not performance or physical-driving evidence. No third accessory or lighting evaluation was performed.
